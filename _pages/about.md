# Katherine Gold

Hi there! My name is Katherine (Kate) Gold, and I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemistry at the University of Arizona, where I work with Prof. Lucy Ziurys. I am broadly interested in astrochemistry, evolved stars, and radio astronomy. My research focuses on understanding how molecular chemistry changes as stars evolve from the asymptotic giant branch through the protoplanetary nebula and planetary nebula phases. I primarily use radio and millimeter/submillimeter telescopes, including the ARO 12 m, Submillimeter Telescope (SMT), IRAM 30 m, and ALMA, to study molecular inventories, isotopic abundances, and the relationship between chemistry and nebular evolution.

My dissertation, Chemistry in Protoplanetary and Planetary Nebulae: Evidence of Nebular Shaping and an Organic Continuum, explores the rich molecular chemistry that persists during the late stages of stellar evolution and what these molecules can tell us about stellar nucleosynthesis, nebular shaping, and the development of chemical complexity. Alongside my research, I am passionate about teaching, mentoring, and making astronomy accessible to students and the public.

Before beginning my Ph.D., I earned my A.B. in Mathematics, magna cum laude, from Bryn Mawr College, with minors in Physics and Astronomy. As an undergraduate, I conducted astronomy research at Swarthmore College and Haverford College, including work on molecular abundances in planetary nebulae and spiral-galaxy morphology.

## Research interests include:

- Observational astrochemistry
- Radio and millimeter/submillimeter astronomy
- Protoplanetary and planetary nebulae
- Circumstellar chemistry
- Molecular spectroscopy
- Molecular and isotopic abundances
- Late-stage stellar evolution
